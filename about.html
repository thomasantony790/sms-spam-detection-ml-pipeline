What the model learns

SMS spam often uses links, short codes, spacing tricks, mixed symbols, and altered spelling. Character n-grams capture those patterns inside the raw text.

Good at
  • Links and domains, even with odd spacing
  • Short codes and phone-like patterns
  • Obfuscated words like fr33, cl1ck, w1n
  • Compact promo phrasing found in SMS
Less reliable on
  • Neutral one-liners with no markers
  • New scam styles with no training examples yet

Processing steps

  1. 1
    Text in
    You paste an SMS message into the analyzer.
  2. 2
    Vectorize
    Character n-grams turn text into TF-IDF features.
  3. 3
    Classify
    Logistic Regression outputs ham or spam, plus a probability score.
  4. 4
    Save label
    You store the correct label for future retraining.

Why this model

Model comparison
  • Word TF-IDF + Multinomial Naive Bayes: solid on obvious promos, weaker on obfuscation.
  • Linear SVM: strong accuracy, needs tuning, probability output varies.
  • Character n-gram TF-IDF + Logistic Regression: best balance on short, noisy SMS, stable probabilities.
Final choice

TF-IDF with character n-grams plus Logistic Regression. Strong signal on short messages. Better handling of altered spelling and links. Stable probabilities for a confidence display.
